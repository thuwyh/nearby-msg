One service request, three perspectives

Alex needs a plumber. Nearby selects professionals and sends updates when the request is submitted, matched and completed.

All three views share one dataset. This system is unfinished; the UI shows actual behavior, which may differ from the requirements.

Quick guide · get started in one minute

Start with one confirmation email

  1. If a previous simulation is saved, choose “Start over”. Submitting the customer request queues an email; it does not send it.
  2. Customer: submit Alex’s request. The platform shows the request and a queued confirmation email.
  3. Platform: send queued messages, open a message, then update its delivery status with a delivered receipt. Switch perspectives to inspect each person’s inbox.
  4. Then try matching: in Platform, select Jordan, Sam or both and confirm the match. A match selects who to notify; it is not a booking or an accepted job.
  5. After marking the service completed, a next-day follow-up is queued. Advance simulated time, change recipient preferences or simulate a send failure to inspect the result.

The UI reflects the current backend, which may omit notifications or send incorrectly. Matching and message delivery are separate; inboxes show only messages the backend marks delivered.

Useful distinctions

Consent to receive notifications
The recipient decides whether Nearby may email or text them. Turning off a channel and saving withdraws consent. “Stop all notifications” overrides both channels. This is separate from staff access permissions.
Service request / job
Alex’s plumbing request, identified as job-1. Submission, matching and completion are three business events for that request.
Professional
Jordan and Sam do the home-service work. The matched list is stored in job.providers.
Messaging gateway
The external email/SMS delivery service, replaced locally by a simulator. Acceptance does not mean delivery; a later receipt reports delivery.

Other controls

  • Customers and professionals manage their own preferences. The platform previews campaigns, creates messages, simulates gateway faults and requests support retries from message details.
  • The clock under “Simulated time & data” does not advance automatically; dispatch after advancing it. Reset clears the request journey, messages, preference changes and gateway records. Refreshing or restarting preserves data.
  • Platform “Advanced debugging” supports raw event replay and fixture editing. Detailed records show actual calls and responses. No real email or SMS is sent.
